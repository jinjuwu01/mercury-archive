# 图像来源与转载说明 / Figure credits and rights

These entries originate from **Optional Supporting Materials.pdf**, supplied by the researcher. The gallery's first three thumbnails were extracted from the PDF and compressed for identification; they are not high-resolution facsimiles. The final item has **no reproduced photograph**, pending confirmation of permission from the modern rightsholders.

## 1. Boym's map of Guizhou
Michael Boym (1612–1659), “Kueycieu” (Guizhou), in *Magni Catay*, ca. 1652–1655, fol. 17r, Borg. cin. 531, Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana. https://digi.vatlib.it/view/MSS_Borg.cin.531. The map marks mercury using “Arg. viu.” and ☿.

## 2. Putting in Mercury
Unknown Cantonese artist, “Putting in Mercury,” *Manufacture of Vermilion in China*, ca. 1820, opaque watercolour, Add.Or.2363, British Library. https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-003271830.

## 3. Wen Shu's images
Wen Shu 文俶, *Jinshi Kunchong Caomu Zhuang* 金石昆蟲草木狀, 1617–1620, manuscript collection no. 06921, National Central Library, Taipei. https://rbook.ncl.edu.tw/NCLSearch/Selection/Details/38. Three scenes: cinnabar from Chenzhou, extracting mercury from cinnabar, and preparing mercury-based powder.

## 4. Wanshan smelting
Yingfu Li et al., “Archaeological Investigation and Industrial Heritage Study of the Wanshan Mercury Mining Site in Guizhou Province, China,” *Industrial Archaeology Review* 45, no. 2 (2023): 74–84, fig. 4 (p. 78), photo credited in the supplied PDF to Chen Shikun / Wanshan Mercury Mine Industrial Heritage Museum. https://doi.org/10.1080/03090728.2023.2257579.
